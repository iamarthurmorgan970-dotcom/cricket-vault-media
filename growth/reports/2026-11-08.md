## What we learned (confidence)

- **Build-up openings lose ~87% of viewers in the first second** (3 videos, fairly sure). The thriller, Kohli v Root
  and Bumrah v Starc all opened on an empty chart or zeros/dashes and kept 12-13%. This is the old template;
  the 3 Nov hook fix was not in these videos.
- **Record cards beat everything so far** (early signal, 2 cards). 23% stayed vs 13%, and the 6s cards loop
  (101% and 200% viewed). Cards are also cheaper to make. Card vs comparison is mixed up with length and
  template, so Philander and Voges (new comparison template, frame-1 hook) are the real test.
- **Posting time: no evidence yet.** All 5 measured videos were at 19:30.
- **Titles and topic: no clear pattern** with 5 videos. India-player videos averaged 488 views; the non-India Rabada card got 1,118.
- **Nobody comments, shares or subscribes yet** (0 of each on 3,068 views). This is now the biggest gap.

## Best and worst

- **Best: Rabada, fewest balls to 350 Test wickets** (1,118 views, 21% stayed, 200% viewed). Frame 1 is the
  finished top-5 table with the headline "Fewest balls to 350 Test wickets" and huge yellow numbers, so the
  whole point lands instantly; at 6 seconds people watched it twice on average. An all-time record ("beat Steyn").
- **Worst: IND v NZ last-over thriller** (324 views, 12% stayed, 53% viewed, 0 likes). Posted a week after the
  match, 17 seconds long, and the worm chart builds up slowly under the hook; viewers left before the payoff.
