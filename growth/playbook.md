# The Cricket Vault: growth playbook

The daily task follows this file when choosing, titling and captioning posts. The weekly review rewrites it
from the channel's real numbers. Keep it short: rules, the evidence for each rule, and what's being tested.

## How the Shorts algorithm judges us (what we optimise)

1. **Stayed rate**: engaged views ÷ views. YouTube counts a "view" from the first frame, so this is the
   share of people who saw the Short in their feed and didn't swipe away. This is the #1 signal.
2. **Average % viewed**: above 80% is good; above 100% means people rewatch (loops).
3. **Engagement per view**: likes, comments, shares, subscribers gained.
4. **Consistency**: one Short a day, at the time our audience is on.

## Current rules

- **The first second decides everything.** Put the crazy number or question in huge text in frame 1.
  No title cards, no empty charts, no zeros counting up before the point is clear.
  Evidence: India vs NZ thriller kept only 38 of 317 feed viewers (12%) as of 3 Nov.
- **Title leads with the number or the question.** "11 off 6 to win… can India do it?" scored 90 on vidIQ,
  the best of anything we've tested.
- **Big gaps beat close calls.** Bumrah vs Starc (avg 19.8 vs 28.3) was our best video: 693 views and
  4 likes in its first day. Close comparisons are parked.
- **Match Shorts must be fresh** (posted within a day of the match). The India vs NZ Short went out a week
  late and stalled at about 320 views, while the same last over went viral elsewhere the day after the game.
- **Every caption ends with a debate question**, and the first comment asks it again (for example
  "Bumrah or Starc: who would you pick?"). We have 0 comments so far, and comments feed the algorithm.
