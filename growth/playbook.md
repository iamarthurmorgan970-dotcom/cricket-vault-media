# The Cricket Vault: growth playbook

The daily task follows this file when choosing, titling and captioning posts. The weekly review rewrites it
from the channel's real numbers. Keep it short: rules, the evidence for each rule, and what's being tested.

## How the Shorts algorithm judges us (what we optimise)

1. **Stayed rate**: engaged views ÷ views. YouTube counts a "view" from the first frame, so this is the
   share of people who saw the Short in their feed and didn't swipe away. This is the #1 signal.
2. **Average % viewed**: above 80% is good; above 100% means people rewatch (loops).
3. **Engagement per view**: likes, comments, shares, subscribers gained.
4. **Consistency**: one Short a day, at the time our audience is on.

94% of our views come from the Shorts feed (2,882 of 3,074 in 28 days), 6% from search. Winning the feed is the job.

## Current rules

- **The whole point must be readable in frame 1.** No empty charts, zeros or dashes counting up.
  Evidence (3 videos): the three 17-20s build-up videos kept only 12-13% of feed viewers; the two
  cards that show the finished ranking at once kept 21% and 30%. Note: Philander and Voges (7, 9 Nov)
  still open on a big "9 vs 1" / "61.9 vs 42.0" over an empty chart; the 6s `versus` card replaces this.
- **Gap days cost views.** Days with no new Short get almost nothing: 6 Nov 37 views, 10 Nov 46,
  vs 200-1,067 on most days a new Short went out (Metricool daily channel views). The every-other-day
  pace since 9 Nov is the likeliest reason views fell this week. Changing the pace is a daily-task
  change: proposed to Deepak in report 15 Nov; until he approves, keep the current pace.
- **Record cards over comparisons.** Comparisons are parked (Deepak, 13 Nov). Evidence (early, 2 cards
  vs 3 others): cards 23% stayed and 100-200% viewed; build-up comparisons 13% and 53-77%.
- **Fill the card: a full top-5 list, not two rows.** Early signal (4 cards, views only): the two
  5-row cards were our biggest days (Rabada 1,118 views; Akash's launch day 515 channel views); the
  two 2-row cards were small (Arshdeep 322; Abhishek's first two days 126 channel views in total).
  A 2-row card leaves half the screen black in frame 1.
- **The number in the hook must be small and instantly felt.** "11 balls", "19 of 20 wickets", "7 all
  out" beat "1,024 balls" or "13,808 balls", which the viewer has to think about. Early signal (Abhishek).
- **Short and loopable wins.** 6-second cards were watched 1-2 times over (Rabada 200%, Arshdeep 101%).
- **The fact must survive "no way!".** Big, simple gaps or all-time records only.
- **Match Shorts must be fresh** (within a day of the match). The week-old thriller was our worst.
- **Every caption ends with a debate question; the first comment asks a different pick-a-side
  question.** Still unproven: comments and shares have been 0 in every measured week.
- **Titles: put the record or the big number in the first 5 words.** House style, not a proven rule.
- **India-only is not required.** The best videos were Rabada (South African) and a Meghalaya
  first-class record; the Abhishek (India T20I) card was the weakest this week.
