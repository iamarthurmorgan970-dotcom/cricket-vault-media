## 5. Choosing today's video

Candidates looked at: 022 Why ships don't sail around South America, 025 Roopkund vs Chadar, 027 True scale of the
solar system, 023 UP vs the world, 020 Sandakphu vs Goechala.

**Chosen: 022 Why Ships Don't Sail Around South America (True Scale world).**

- **Balance:** three India videos in a row; a world "why" video rebalances the test, and 007 (the last world "why")
  is now a week old.
- **News hook (this month):** on 9 Nov 2026 the Panama Canal raised daily slots from 33 to 34 and restored the full
  50-foot Neopanamax draft as water levels recovered (Marine Insight, 12 Nov). On 21 Oct it reported a record
  521 million tons for fiscal 2026 (13,951 transits, +4.1%) (Maritime Executive, Seatrade). The video ends on this
  "the water came back" story after the 2023-24 drought.
- **Season:** the Antarctic cruise season (Nov-Mar) is when Drake Passage and Cape Horn crossings, and searches about
  them, peak; Drake Passage crossing videos are a known curiosity genre.
- **Search demand (general, not channel data):** "why don't ships go around South America", "Cape Horn",
  "Drake Passage" and "how the Panama Canal works" are evergreen curiosity searches.
- **True-scale angle:** New York to San Francisco, about 13,000 miles around South America vs about 5,200 through
  Panama (Britannica), animated on the map.
- Why not the others: 025 Roopkund vs Chadar fits mid-December, closer to the January Chadar season. 027 Solar system
  is evergreen with no timing reason. 023 would be a second India-vs-World data video before 013 has numbers. 020 is
  a second trek comparison before 005 has numbers.
- One subject: the sea route around the bottom of South America and why ships avoid it (Cape Horn, Drake Passage,
  Magellan, and the Panama shortcut that replaced them). Not merged with any other plan item.

Applied (general, not channel-proven): a single number contrast in the first sentence (13,000 vs 5,200 miles), a
three-question promise in the first 20 seconds, the route drawn on the map in the hook, one fresh news beat near the
end, and a "which would you pick" comment prompt (Cape Horn for the earring, or the Panama shortcut?).
Thumbnail: 3 huge words over a map of South America's tip with the route.

Accuracy notes: Wikipedia's Panama Canal page could not be fetched this run, so all canal figures come from
Britannica (length, 26 m lift, lock steps, 197 million litres per transit, 8-10 hours, ~25,600 deaths, 1881/1904/1914
dates, NY-SF miles), the Panama Canal Authority (2021 max length, 96.8%), gCaptain (51.25 m beam, CMA CGM Theodore
Roosevelt), MercoPress/FreightWaves/McKinsey (drought) and Seatrade/Maritime Executive/Marine Insight (2026).
Drake Passage width differs between sources (Wikipedia ~800 km between Cape Horn and Livingston Island; Britannica
~1,000 km); the video says "about 800 km at its narrowest" and shows Britannica's figure on screen. Trade share
differs (McKinsey ~2.5%, MercoPress ~5%); the video gives the range. Kilometre figures for NY-SF are converted from
Britannica's miles. Routes, current arrows and the locks profile are schematic and labelled as such.
