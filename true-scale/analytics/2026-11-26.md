## 5. Choosing today's video

Candidates looked at: 021 Living root bridges, 011 Majuli, 025 Roopkund vs Chadar, 027 Solar system, 020 Sandakphu
vs Goechala.

**Chosen: 021 Meghalaya's Bridges That Grow (Why You Should Visit).**

- **Broad curiosity, works for India and the world:** "a bridge made from a living tree" needs no prior knowledge of
  Meghalaya, unlike most place videos; the title and thumbnail ("THIS BRIDGE IS ALIVE") can carry the click on
  their own. Living root bridges are a well-known internet curiosity (Smithsonian, NPR, Atlas Obscura coverage).
- **Timely hook:** India submitted the UNESCO World Heritage nomination ("Jingkieng Jri") in January 2026 for the
  2026-27 evaluation cycle (All India Radio News, 29 Jan 2026; Lok Sabha reply reported 23 Mar 2026), so the
  evaluation is under way now. The video doesn't claim a decision date.
- **Season:** Meghalaya Tourism gives October to March as the dry-season window for the Nongriat trek, so winter
  trip-planning searches are live now.
- **Balance:** yesterday was world (022); an India place video with global appeal keeps the mix even.
- Why not the others: 011 Majuli has a strong erosion story but a narrower audience; keep it for a later Northeast
  slot (and don't run two Northeast places back to back). 025 Roopkund vs Chadar fits mid-December, nearer the
  January Chadar season. 027 Solar system has no timing reason. 020 would be a second trek comparison before 005
  has numbers.
- One subject: the living root bridges of the Khasi and Jaintia Hills (how they're grown, the 2019 survey, the
  Nongriat double-decker, caretakers, threats, UNESCO nomination, how to visit). Not merged with any other plan item.

Applied (general, not channel-proven): a visual "this is alive" claim in the first sentence with three on-screen
proof cards; a four-point promise in the first 20 seconds; data charts from the 2019 Scientific Reports survey
(lengths, caretakers) to keep the True Scale identity; a live news beat near the end; a "would you trust it?"
comment prompt.

Accuracy notes: core figures come from Ludwig et al., Scientific Reports 2019 (76 bridges inventoried; lengths
2-52.7 m; 58 of 73 measured lengths under 20 m; caretakers 25 village / 12 family / 8 consortium / 14 unknown /
16 untended = 75; one bridge not fully functional after 66 years; ages only rough estimates). Build time differs by
source (Wikipedia Cherrapunji 10-15 years, Wikipedia Living root bridge up to 15, UNDP 20-25), so the video says
"about ten to twenty-five". Load "fifty or more people" is from Wikipedia and is said as "reportedly". Smithsonian's
"nearly 173 metres" longest-length figure conflicts with the paper and was not used. Rainfall: Wikipedia
(Cherrapunji) averages 11,777 mm Sohra / 11,873 mm Mawsynram (Britannica's Sohra average is about 11,430 mm, shown
on screen); records from Guinness World Records and WMO (26,461 mm Aug 1860-Jul 1861; 9,300 mm July 1861). Step
counts differ (3,000 / 3,500 / 2,600 down + 500 up), so the video says "thousands... around 3,500 by many counts"
and shows 3,000-3,500. Double-decker deck lengths (80 and 60 ft) are from Smithsonian, converted. Map pins are
approximate; all landscapes are illustrations.
