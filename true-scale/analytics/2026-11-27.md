**Chosen: 037 How Big Are the Oceans, Really? (True Scale, world).**

- **Balance:** yesterday was India (021); the run alternates India/world while there is no data to say which wins.
- **Proven-elsewhere format, our core identity:** "how big is X really" is the channel's own name and the same
  pattern as 017 Antarctica, so 037 gives the first like-for-like pair to compare once Studio numbers exist.
  "Everest would fit in the Mariana Trench" is a widely shared fact with broad, non-regional curiosity.
- **Timely beats (all 2026, all verified this run):** Seabed 2030 announced 28.7% of the seafloor mapped (IHO,
  28 Apr 2026); the High Seas Treaty entered into force on 17 Jan 2026 with its first COP due within a year; an
  October 2026 report of 57 new species from the Mar del Plata canyon off Argentina; the Ocean Census Alliance's
  1,121 new species in a year.
- **Why not the others:** 032 empires needs reliable historical outlines and peak-area sources (more research than
  one run can verify; keep for a day with time to source each empire). 027 solar system has no timing reason. 038
  Bhimbetka and 011 Majuli are India picks for the next India slot. 025 Roopkund vs Chadar fits mid-December, closer
  to the January Chadar season.
- One subject: the world ocean and its five oceans (area, volume, depth, trenches, mapping, ownership). Not merged
  with any other plan item.

Applied (general, not channel-proven): a single visual number contrast in the first sentence (Everest in the
trench), a three-part promise inside 20 seconds, equal-area maps with true-shape moves (India, Australia, China,
USA dropped into the Pacific), and a three-option comment prompt.

Accuracy notes. Research web fetches were partly blocked this run (Wikipedia, NOAA Ocean Service, USGS and most
Britannica pages timed out on permission), so the video uses only pages that were actually read:
- Britannica, "Major subdivisions of the oceans" (table from NOAA/Eakins & Sharman 2010): areas Pacific 168.723,
  Atlantic 85.133, Indian 70.560, Southern (south of 60°S) 21.960, Arctic 15.558, total 361.934 million km²;
  volumes 669.880 / 310.411 / 264.000 / 71.800 / 18.750, total 1,335 million km³; average depths 3,970 / 3,646 /
  3,741 / 3,270 / 1,205 m, world 3,688 m; 70.8% of the surface below sea level; Mariana Trench 11,034 m; Everest
  8,850 m.
- Webb, Introduction to Oceanography (CC BY; RWU Pressbooks / LibreTexts): 71% ocean; 61% of the Northern and 81%
  of the Southern Hemisphere; 97% of surface water in the oceans (Pacific 52, Atlantic 25, Indian 20), ice 2%,
  groundwater 0.6%, lakes/rivers/air 0.01%; mean depth "about 3,800 m"; Challenger Deep 10,920 m (RWU copy) vs
  11,022 m (LibreTexts copy). The video therefore says "about 10,900 to just over 11,000 m" and "about 2 km" of
  water above Everest (2.07-2.18 km).
- Derived (stated as such): all land = 29.2/70.8 × 361.9 ≈ 149.3 million km²; Pacific minus land ≈ 19.4 ("about
  20 million to spare"); Pacific share 46.6%; 3,688/8,850 ≈ two fifths; Arctic 1,205/3,688 ≈ one third; time to
  map the rest at 5 million km²/yr ≈ 51 years ("roughly fifty").
- IHO (28 Apr 2026): 28.7% mapped, about 104 million km², almost 5 million km² added in a year.
- Scientific American (Ocean Census): 1,121 new species, 728 from existing collections, 13 expeditions and nine
  workshops, more than 13 years from collection to description, under 0.001% of the seafloor directly seen.
- Discover Wildlife (3 Oct 2026): 57 new species, Mar del Plata Canyon, more than 3,500 m deep.
- WRI (15 Jan 2026): roughly two thirds of the ocean outside national jurisdiction; under 1% of high seas in MPAs.
  EU Commission (16 Jan 2026): in force 17 Jan 2026; COP within a year.
- India's area was not stated (the official source couldn't be fetched); India appears only as a true-shape move.
  Ocean colours on the maps are a simplified classification and pins (Challenger Deep, Mar del Plata Canyon) are
  approximate.
