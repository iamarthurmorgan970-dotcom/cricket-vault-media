## 5. Choosing today's video

Candidates looked at: 017 How big is Antarctica really?, 016 Gandikota, 013 Indian states vs countries,
040 Har Ki Dun vs Deoriatal-Chandrashila, 021 Living root bridges.

**Chosen: 017 How Big Is Antarctica Really? (True Scale, world).**

- **Balance:** the last three were India place (036), world (007), India heritage (018). A world video keeps the mix
  even while we wait for data on which side the audience prefers.
- **The channel's own format:** "how big is X really" with true-size moves is exactly what the channel name promises,
  and only 001 has used it so far. Antarctica is the clearest case of map distortion (Mercator turns it into a strip
  across the whole bottom of the map), so the before/after is strong in the first 20 seconds.
- **News hook:** NASA and NSIDC confirmed that Antarctic sea ice peaked on 14 Sep 2026 at about 17.59M km², the
  third-lowest winter maximum on record, and NSIDC reported record daily lows from early October. Timely, verified,
  and a natural part of "how big", since the frozen sea grows bigger than the continent.
- **Season:** November is the start of the southern summer, when Antarctica tourism and Antarctic expeditions
  (including India's annual expedition) are in the news.
- **India angle for the core audience:** India and Australia dropped onto Antarctica at true size, "more than four
  Indias" in the thumbnail, and a short section on Dakshin Gangotri, Maitri and Bharati.
- Why not the others: 016 Gandikota and 021 root bridges are good India place picks for next week (Gandikota's camping
  season runs Oct-Feb); 013 needs a big batch of verified state/country pairings and is better as its own careful
  run; 040 is a second trek comparison before 005 has data.
- One subject: Antarctica's true size (area, ice, water, cold, sea ice, people, claims). No other plan item merged.

Applied (general, not channel-proven): a verified size contrast in the first sentence ("bigger than India and Australia
put together"), the Mercator vs globe reveal at 1 minute, true-size moves of India and Australia, an India
reference in the thumbnail ("ANTARCTICA 4× INDIA"), and a three-way comment question (size, ice or cold?).

Accuracy notes: area 14.2M km², "about 40% larger than Europe, almost double Australia", 1.9 km average ice,
about 70% of fresh water, -89.2 °C (Vostok, 21 Jul 1983), 150 mm / under 50 mm precipitation, 5,000 summer / 1,000
winter population, 70+ stations: Wikipedia, Antarctica. Ice volume 27.17M km³, max thickness 4,757 m, 58 m sea-level
equivalent: Bedmap3 (Pritchard et al. 2025, Scientific Data). Australia 7,688,287 km²: Geoscience Australia; India
3,287,263 km²: Embassy of India factsheet (used in 012). Leftover 14.2 - 3.29 - 7.69 = 3.22M km². Satellite -98 °C:
AGU press release (2018). Sea ice: NASA Earth Observatory and NSIDC (Sep 2026), Nature World News (Oct 2026) for the
October record daily lows. Lake Vostok (250 x 50 km, ~4 km under ice, 1993, Feb 2012, ~15 million years): Wikipedia.
Tourists 118,491 in 2024-25: MercoPress citing IAATO. Seven claimants and the Argentina/Chile/UK overlap: Wikipedia;
Australia's claim ~42% and its 45°E-160°E sectors (minus Adélie Land 136-142°E): Australian Antarctic Division.
Treaty 1959, 58 parties (2024): Wikipedia; India acceded 1 Aug 1983, first expedition 1981, Dakshin Gangotri 1983
(retired 1990), Maitri 1989, Bharati 2012: Wikipedia, Indian Antarctic Programme. Burj Khalifa 828 m is the standard
published height. The polar maps use an equal-area projection; the outline includes ice shelves (Natural Earth,
about 13.8M km² as drawn); the sea-ice belt and Lake Vostok outline are simplified and labelled so.
