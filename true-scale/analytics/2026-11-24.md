## 5. Choosing today's video

Candidates looked at: 028 Hampi boulders, 022 Why ships don't sail around South America, 025 Roopkund vs Chadar,
023 UP vs the world (population), 020 Sandakphu vs Goechala.

**Chosen: 028 Why Hampi Is Covered in Giant Boulders (India's Monoliths & Rock Wonders).**

- **News hook (this month):** on 14 Nov 2026 the Archaeological Survey of India reported the remains of two
  Vijayanagara-era temples (Vishnu and Shiva) near the Ganagitti Jain temple at Hampi (IANS). In May 2026 it also
  found a buried temple ceiling with elephant carvings near the Hazara Rama temple. Fresh finds give the video a
  "still being dug up" segment and a reason for news searches to surface it.
- **Season:** Karnataka Tourism recommends October to February; late November to January is peak Hampi trip-planning
  time for Indian viewers, so "Hampi" searches should be near their yearly high.
- **Search demand (general, not channel data):** Hampi is one of India's best-known heritage destinations and
  "why is Hampi full of boulders" is a classic curiosity question; the boulder geology is rarely explained well.
- **Series test:** the Monoliths & Rock Wonders series has one episode (018 Kailasa). A second, different-format
  episode (a geology "why" question) gives two data points for the series once numbers arrive.
- **True-scale angle:** the granite's roughly 2.5 billion years squeezed into one day makes the whole Vijayanagara
  reign last about 0.008 seconds (arithmetic: 229 / 2,500,000,000 × 86,400 s).
- Why not the others: 022 South America is a good world "why" topic but its Panama Canal news (traffic back to 34
  daily slots) is a softer hook, and 007 already tested the world-"why" format a week ago; keep it for next week. 025
  Roopkund vs Chadar is better in mid-December, closer to the January Chadar season. 023 would be a second
  India-vs-World data video straight after 013; wait for 013's numbers. 020 is a second trek comparison before 005
  has any numbers.
- One subject: Hampi, told through its boulders (geology, then the city built among them, its fall, today's finds and
  how to visit). Not merged with any other plan item.

Applied (general, not channel-proven): a visual curiosity hook in the first sentence ("nobody dropped them"), the
single most surprising verified number in the first 20 seconds (2.5-billion-year-old granite; second-largest city
around 1500, after Beijing), a four-point promise, a step-by-step geology explainer, a "true scale" clock moment,
and a debate comment prompt ("the legend or the geology?"). Thumbnail: 3 huge words ("WHO PILED THESE?") over one
big boulder heap with a temple tower for scale.

Accuracy notes: Wikipedia's Dharwar Craton page and the UNESCO site could not be fetched this run. UNESCO figures
(1986, 1999-2006, 4,187.24 ha, "more than 1,600 surviving remains") come from the Wikipedia Hampi infobox and lead,
which cite UNESCO; the Wikipedia body also says "41.5 square kilometres", so the video says "about 42 km²". The
granite age (about 2.5 billion years) is from a geology blog (Navrang India, Feb 2026) and agrees with the Late
Archaean age in Moyen et al. 2003 (Closepet Granite, J. Geol. Soc. India); it is said as "roughly". How long the
boulders took to form is disputed between sources (10,000-100,000 years for the weathering stage vs "tens of
millions" of years), so the video says it is "still debated". Paes' Rome comparison is reported, not quoted
(Wikipedia paraphrase). The Tungabhadra river line and the Talikota battle location are approximate and labelled.
