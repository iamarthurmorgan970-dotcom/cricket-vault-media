DESCRIPTION
Europe and Africa are only 13-14 km apart at the Strait of Gibraltar, closer than the sea section of Mumbai's Atal
Setu. Yet there is no bridge, no tunnel and no road between the two continents. Why?

In this video: the strait at its true scale (about 58 km long, 13-14 km at its narrowest), the 100,000+ ships that
pass every year, water up to about 900 m deep, why a bridge would need a span 2.5 times the world record, the two
currents stacked on top of each other, the moving plates and the clay-filled channels under the seabed, the
"Zanclean flood" that refilled the Mediterranean about 5.33 million years ago, nearly 100 years of plans, and the
railway tunnel Spain and Morocco are studying now: 65 km long, about 28 km under the sea, up to about 475 m below sea
level. Plus what it costs, when it could open, and why it won't be ready for the 2030 World Cup.

Would you take a 30-minute train under the sea between two continents, or stick with the ferry? Tell me in the
comments.
