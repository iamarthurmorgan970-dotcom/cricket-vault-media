DESCRIPTION
For part of every year, a huge stretch of western India is a shallow wetland. Then the water dries up and leaves
a flat, white desert of salt, and on an island in the middle sits a city planned about 5,000 years ago.

This is the Rann of Kutch in Gujarat, mapped to true scale: how big it really is (sources disagree, and we show
both figures), why it was once part of the Arabian Sea, how the monsoon and the sun make the white salt crust,
Flamingo City in the Kutch Desert Wildlife Sanctuary, the 1965 border fight and the 1968 tribunal, Rann Utsav
2026-27 at Dhordo (1 Nov 2026 to 7 Mar 2027) and this season's full-moon dates, the view from Kalo Dungar, and
Dholavira: the Harappan city on Khadir Bet with its stone reservoirs, giant stepwell and the "world's oldest known
signboard". Plus how to get there from Bhuj, and what to check before you go.

Which would you go for first: the white desert under a full moon, or the 5,000-year-old city? Tell me in the
comments.
