DESCRIPTION
Sail from New York to San Francisco around the bottom of South America and it's about 13,000 miles. Cut through
Panama and it's about 5,200. So why did Cape Horn become the most feared corner of the sailing world, and what
happens when the shortcut runs out of water?

In this video: Cape Horn and the Drake Passage at their true scale (about 800 km wide, about 3,400 m deep on
average), the Antarctic Circumpolar Current (an estimated 100-150 million cubic metres of water a second, several
times the Gulf Stream), winter gales, 12 m waves and rogue waves up to 30 m, the Bounty's 31 days and 85 miles in
1788, the clipper ships and the gold earring, the twisting 570 km Strait of Magellan, how the Panama Canal lifts
ships 26 m over a country on rain water, the 2016 locks for ships up to about 370 m long, the 2023 drought that cut
crossings from about 36 a day to 22, and the November 2026 recovery to 34 daily slots after a record year.

Would you round Cape Horn to earn the gold earring, or take the shortcut through Panama? Tell me in the comments.
