DESCRIPTION
About 500 km east of Mumbai there's a hole in the ground almost two kilometres wide, made by a rock from space.
And right now the lake at its bottom is rising so fast that centuries-old temples are going under water.

This is Lonar crater lake in Buldhana district, Maharashtra, at its true scale: how big and deep it really is
(drawn to scale), the meteorite that made it, why it's often described as the only known impact crater formed
entirely in basalt (and why that links it to the Moon and Mars), the debate over its age (about 52,000 or about
570,000 years?), the salty, alkaline lake that turned pink in June 2020, its temples and wildlife, the rising
water that has submerged at least six temples, and how and when to visit this winter.

Should Maharashtra pump water out of Lonar to save the temples, or leave the lake to nature? Tell me in the
comments.
