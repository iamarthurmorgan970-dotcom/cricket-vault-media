DESCRIPTION
This bridge is alive. Nobody built it from wood, steel or concrete: it was grown, root by root, from a living rubber
fig, and unlike every other bridge, it gets stronger as it gets older. One scientific survey catalogued 76 of them
in the Khasi and Jaintia Hills of Meghalaya, India.

In this video: where they are (Sohra/Cherrapunji and Mawsynram, rivals for "wettest place on Earth", with Sohra's
world records of about 26,461 mm of rain in 12 months and 9,300 mm in July 1861), why wood and steel fail in these
valleys, the rubber fig (Ficus elastica) and inosculation, how a bridge is grown step by step, why it takes about 10
to 25 years, why it gets stronger with age, what the 2019 Scientific Reports survey found (lengths from 2 m to
52.7 m, who looks after them, 16 untended), the famous Nongriat double-decker, the threats, India's 2026 UNESCO
World Heritage nomination, and how to visit (October to March, Tyrna, thousands of steps, Rainbow Falls).

Would you trust your weight to a bridge made of a living tree? Tell me in the comments.
